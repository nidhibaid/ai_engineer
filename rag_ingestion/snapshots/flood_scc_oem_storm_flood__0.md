# Storm and Flood Safety

Storm clouds

Storms and floods can bring heavy rain, strong winds, and rising water. These conditions can cause power outages, landslides, and property damage. Knowing what to do before, during, and after a storm or flood can help keep you safe.

## What is El Niño?

El Niño is a climate pattern that happens irregularly every few years. It typically lasts a few months to over a year.

It can change the weather a lot and increase the chance of storms in Santa Clara County. It does not guarantee bad weather, but it can make heavy rain and strong storms more likely. One big storm can cause a lot of damage, even if the rest of the winter ends up normal or dry.

[The National Weather Service says a strong El Niño will likely last through early 2027](https://www.noaa.gov/news-release/el-nino-looms-large-over-us-winter-outlook) . Northern California will likely get more rain than usual this winter.

Map of North America showing that, during El Niño, an extended Pacific jet stream brings increased storms and wetter conditions to Northern California, while the northern U.S. is warmer and the Southeast is drier.

Image from NOAAClimate.gov

### Why Does this Happen?

Normally, winds blow warm water across the ocean from South America to Asia. Cold water then comes up from deep in the ocean. Ocean temperature influences the weather throughout the year.

But during El Niño, this _changes_ .

During El Niño, winds aren't strong enough to push warm water away from South America. This leaves warm water in place and affects the weather.

### What Does it Mean for Santa Clara County?

Santa Clara County does not see big changes as often as other parts of California. During El Niño, this area can have:

* **Storms** — rain is heavy and lasts a long time.
* **Floods** — creeks, rivers, and the coast overflow.
* **Landslides** — wet soil slides down hills.

#### Before a Storm or Flood

* **Check weather alerts** from the [National Weather Service](https://www.weather.gov/mtr/) .
* [**Know your flood risk**](https://www.valleywater.org/floodzone) . Find out whether your home or workplace is in an area that can flood.
  
    + Be careful around areas downhill from recent wildfires. After a fire, heavy rain can cause floods, mudslides, or landslides.
* [**Get sandbags**](https://www.valleywater.org/flooding-safety/flood-ready/sandbags) to slow or divert floodwater away from your property.
* [**Prepare for power outages**](https://oem.santaclaracounty.gov/prepare/disaster-preparedness/power-outage-safety) **.**
* **Prepare your property.**
* **Secure or put away items** that may blow away or fly through a window.
* **Charge your electronic devices.**
* **Save copies of important documents.** Save these copies online and in a waterproof bag. This includes birth certificates, passports, medical records, and insurance papers.
* **Drive safely** and avoid travel if you can.
* **Be ready** and remind neighbors to be ready to:
  
    + Leave if told to do so.
    + Move to higher ground or a higher floor.
    + Stay where you are.
* [**Get or review your flood insurance**](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance) **.** Flood insurance typically takes 30 days to start.

**Quick Tip 💡:** Take pictures and videos of the important items in your home. They will help if you need to file an insurance claim.

#### During a Storm or Flood

* **Listen to emergency alerts.** Follow evacuation orders and warnings .
* **Avoid travel** if you can.
* **Watch for falling trees and downed power lines** . Do not touch downed power lines. [Report them to your electric company](https://www.pge.com/en/contact-us/report-an-issue/report-electric-issue.html) .
* **Find a safe shelter right away.**
* **During a flood:**
  
    + Stay away from creeks, rivers, and floodwater.
    + Do not walk or drive through floodwater.
    + Stay off bridges over fast-moving water.
    + Get to the highest floor if you are stuck in a building. Only go onto the roof if you must, and once you’re there, try to signal for help.
    + Do not climb into a closed attic, because rising water can trap you inside.
* **During strong winds:**
  
    + Take cover inside a building.
    + Stay away from windows.
    + Use handrails on outdoor walkways when you can.
    + Avoid high places, like roofs.

#### After a Storm or Flood

* **Return home only when officials say it is safe.**
* **Do not touch anything electric** if you are wet or standing in water.
* **Watch for falling trees and downed power lines** . Do not touch downed power lines. [Report them to your electric company](https://www.pge.com/en/contact-us/report-an-issue/report-electric-issue.html) .
* **Do not walk or drive in floodwater.** It may be dirty, hide sharp objects, or be charged by downed power lines.
* **Listen to news reports to confirm the water is safe to drink.** Drinking water may be dirty.
* **Safely use generators or gasoline‑powered machines** . Keep them outside and at least 20 feet from windows, doors, and vents. Carbon monoxide fumes can kill you.

### Prepare Your Property

Preparing your property before a big storm or flood can help keep you safe and prevent damage.

* Clean out rain gutters and drains of debris. Point them away from your property.
* Trim loose branches and leaning trees around your property.
* Raise furniture on the first floor off the floor or move it to a higher place.
* Seal cracks in the foundation, exterior walls, and small openings around pipes.
* Check that shingles are in good shape.
* Paint exposed wood to prevent rot.
* Cover bare dirt on slopes with a tarp or plant plants.
* Fix small cracks in pavement before they get worse.
* [Learn how to safely turn off gas and electricity](https://www.pge.com/en/outages-and-safety/safety/natural-disaster-safety.html) at the main switches or valves. Unplug electrical items.

#### Clean and Fix Your Property After Damage

* Clean and disinfect everything wet. Throw away items that soak up water—like mattresses, carpets, and drywall.
* Dry your property thoroughly after a flood. It may need mold treatment.
* Wear strong gloves, protective clothes, and boots when cleaning. Wear an N95 mask or respirator if you are cleaning [mold or dirty debris](https://www.cdc.gov/mold-health/communication-resources/guide-to-mold-cleanup.html) .
  
    + People with asthma, lung problems, or weak immune systems should not go into buildings with leaks or mold. Children should not help with disaster cleanup.
* [Fix any broken septic or sewage systems](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/septic-systems-what-do-after-flood) as soon as you can. Damaged sewage systems can be very dangerous and can make people sick.
* Look out for snakes and other animals that may have entered your property.

**Do not drive during a storm or flood** unless it is an emergency.

### Drive Safely

If you must travel, remember these safety tips:

* Check weather alerts and road closures before you leave.
* Give road crews lots of space so they can work safely. Keep roads clear for first responders to get through.
* Never drive around roadblocks. Roads may be damaged.
* Leave extra space between you and the car in front.
* Keep both hands on the wheel and slow down to a safe driving speed.
* During a flood:
  
    + Never drive through floodwater—even 6 inches can cause you to lose control. 12 inches of water can push a car.
    + Stay off bridges over fast-moving water.
    + Do not drive into underground roads or tunnels.
    + If your car is trapped in rising water, call 9-1-1 and stay inside. If water starts coming into the car, climb onto the roof and signal for help.
* During strong winds:
  
    + Watch for debris that may suddenly blow onto the roadway.
    + Stay clear of tall vehicles, including trucks and buses.

## Thunderstorm Safety

Santa Clara County gets only a few thunderstorms each year. Here are more tips to help you stay safe during a thunderstorm:

**Indoor Lightning Safety**  
When you hear thunder, go indoors right away and stay there for 30 minutes after the last sound of thunder.

* Do not use corded phones, computers, or other devices plugged into the wall.
* Unplug appliances or other devices.
* Stay away from sinks, baths, and faucets.
* Keep away from windows, doors, and porches.
* Do not lie on concrete floors or walls. Lightning can travel through metal wires or bars.
* Protect your pets. Remove metal collars, leashes, or harnesses and replace them with non-metal ones.

**If You Are Outside With No Safe Shelter**  
These are steps to stay safe outside:

* Avoid open spaces, vehicles, or structures.
* If possible, get in a hard-topped vehicle with metal top and sides.
* Do not touch anything metal.
* Get off high places, such as hills or mountaintops.
* Do not lie flat on the ground.
* Do not stand under a tree.
* Do not hide under cliffs or rock overhangs.
* Move away from lakes, ponds, or any water. Get to land.
* Stay away from anything that carries electricity, like fences or power lines.

## Understanding Alerts and Warnings

It’s important to know what the alerts and warnings mean. Different alerts tell you how serious the situation is and what you should do to stay safe.

#### Warning

A Warning weather alert means dangerous weather is happening now or will happen soon. It can harm people or damage property.

Examples:

* High Wind Warning
* Severe Thunderstorm Warning
* Flood Warning
* Flash Flood Warning
* River Flood Warning

**Quick Tip** 💡: This is not the same as an Evacuation Warning. See Evacuation Warning to learn more about evacuation alerts.

* * *

The National Weather Service sends out this alert. [Learn more about weather alerts](https://www.weather.gov/mtr/) .

#### Advisory

An Advisory weather alert means bad weather is happening now or will happen soon. It is less serious than a warning. It can still cause problems. If you are not careful, it could become dangerous.

Examples:

* Wind Advisory
* Flood Advisory

* * *

The National Weather Service sends out this alert. [Learn more about weather alerts](https://www.weather.gov/mtr/) .

#### Watch

A Watch weather alert means bad weather _might_ happen. However, we don't know exactly when or where it will occur. It lets people know to be ready.

Examples:

* High Wind Watch
* Severe Thunderstorm Watch
* Flood Watch
* Flash Flood Watch
* River Flood Watch

* * *

The National Weather Service sends out this alert. [Learn more about weather alerts](https://www.weather.gov/mtr/) .

#### Evacuation Warning

An evacuation warning means danger _may_ be nearby. If you get an evacuation warning, you should get your family, pets, and important items ready to leave. If you need more time to leave, you should start leaving now. Be prepared to go to a safe place.

**Quick Tip** 💡: This is not the same as a weather alert warning. See Warning to learn more about weather alerts.

* * *

#### Evacuation Order

An evacuation order means there is _serious_ danger right now. You must leave immediately. The area is closed to the public once this order is given. If you need help leaving, call 9‑1‑1.
