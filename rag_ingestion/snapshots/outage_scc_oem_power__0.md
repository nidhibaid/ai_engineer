# Power Outage Safety

A PG&E Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) occurs in response to severe weather. Learn more about what to expect and how to plan for a power outage.

## Prepare For a Power Outage

A person checking his or her electrical panel in the dark with a flashlight

During months when the risk of wildfire is high or periods of extended drought, Santa Clara County is at risk of wildfire and a Public Safety Power Shutoff. Weather and fire conditions are subject to change, so everybody needs to be ready. Several important ways to prepare for emergencies are:

1. Sign up for AlertSCC, the County’s official alert and warning system.
2. Make a plan.
3. Build an emergency supply kit.
4. Help family, friends and neighbors.

[Sign up for Emergency Alerts](https://oem.santaclaracounty.gov/sign-up-for-alerts)

## PG&E Public Safety Power Shutoff

A PG&E Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) occurs in response to severe weather. PG&E turns off power to help prevent wildfires and keep communities safe. Although you may not live or work in a high fire-threat area or an area experiencing high winds, your power may be shut off if your home or business relies on a line that runs through an area with extreme weather. Power lines aren't always connected according to neighborhood, so your power could stay on while a neighborhood across the street could be turned off.

* During a Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) event, PG&E will establish Community Resource Centers (CRCs) to provide essential services and basic resources to impacted residents. CRCs may provide:
    + Indoor CRC:
        - Wi-fi
        - Restrooms
        - Bottled water
        - Device charging
        - Snacks
        - Blankets
        - Seating
        - Cooling/Heating
        - Ice
    + Outdoor CRC
        - Wi-fi
        - Restrooms
        - Bottled water
        - Device charging
        - Snacks
        - Blankets
        - Seating
* For more information, visit <https://www.pge.com/en/outages-and-safety/outage-preparedness-and-support/general-outage-resources/community-resource-centers.html>

## Enhanced powerline safety settings

To help prevent wildfires during an ongoing drought, PG&E is continuing to evolve our safety efforts to protect our customers and communities. This includes adjusting the sensitivity on some equipment to automatically turn off power within one-tenth of a second if the system detects a problem. We call this Enhanced Powerline Safety Settings (EPSS). This year, we are expanding these safety settings to all powerlines in high fire-risk areas (HFRAs) and some lines that are adjacent to HFRAs. For more information, please visit [pge.com/epss](https://www.pge.com/en/outages-and-safety/safety/community-wildfire-safety-program/enhanced-powerline-safety-settings.html) .

## For PG&E customers

* Stay informed on the latest [Public Safety Power Shutoff updates](http://bit.ly/PSPS-faq) .
* Update contact information [online](https://www.pge.com/en_US/residential/your-account/account-management/manage-your-account/alerts-and-notifications/update-your-contact-information.page) or by calling [1 (866) 743-6589](tel:+1-866-743-6589) during normal business hours. PG&E will use this information to alert customers when and where possible before turning off electric service for safety.
* If you or a household member requires a medical or life support device for medical conditions, apply for the [PG&E Medical Baseline Program](https://www.pge.com/en/account/billing-and-assistance/financial-assistance/medical-baseline-program.html) .
* Prepare for and practice an emergency plan to be emergency-ready and safe during an outage. Keep in mind family members who are elderly, younger children, and pets. Information and tips, including a [safety plan checklist](https://www.pge.com/en_US/residential/outages/public-safety-power-shuttoff/learn-about-psps.page) , are available.
* Emergency supply kits can be a lifesaver during a power outage, earthquake, wildfire, flood, or other disasters. See the [emergency supply checklist](http://bit.ly/em-supplies-PSPS) for a Public Safety Power Shutoff.
